# Oil shock → Indian sectors

India imports roughly 85% of its crude. So a sustained rise in Brent works through
four channels:
**input costs** (fuel, petrochemical raw materials), **inflation → RBI rates**, **rupee**
(bigger import bill → weaker INR), and **FII flows** (risk-off, wider current account deficit).

The short-term reaction is often sharper than the real earnings impact. Most of these
businesses pass costs on with a 1–2 quarter lag. Long-term holders should ask whether the
spike lasts long enough to dent full-year earnings.

## Usually hurt by higher crude

| Sector | Example NSE symbols | Mechanism | Lag / nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oil marketing (OMCs) | IOC, BPCL, HINDPETRO | Pump prices often frozen by government → marketing losses | Refining margins can offset partly; policy risk dominates |
| Aviation | INDIGO, SPICEJET | ATF ≈ 30–40% of costs, tracks crude closely | Fares rise with a lag; airspace closures in Gulf add costs |
| Paints | ASIANPAINT, BERGEPAINT, KANSAINER, INDIGOPNTS | Crude derivatives ≈ large share of raw materials | Price hikes in 1–2 quarters; demand slowdown risk |
| Tyres | MRF, APOLLOTYRE, CEATLTD, JKTYRE | Synthetic rubber, carbon black are crude-linked | Similar 1–2 quarter lag |
| Chemicals / plastics users | PIDILITIND, SUPREMEIND, ASTRAL | Petrochemical feedstock (VAM, PVC) | Strong brands pass on faster |
| Logistics / transport | CONCOR, TCIEXP, DELHIVERY | Diesel costs | Fuel surcharges with lag |
| Autos (demand side) | MARUTI, M&M, HEROMOTOCO | Fuel cost hurts demand; inflation → slower rate cuts | EVs/CNG relatively better |
| Fertilisers | CHAMBLFERT, COROMANDEL, RCF | Gas/feedstock costs; subsidy timing | Government subsidy absorbs much of it |
| Rate-sensitives | Banks, NBFCs, real estate | Inflation → RBI holds/hikes; FII selling | Indirect but broad |

## Usually helped (or relatively defensive)

| Sector | Example NSE symbols | Mechanism | Nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream oil & gas | ONGC, OIL | Realised crude price ↑ | Windfall tax risk caps upside |
| Refiners with export exposure | RELIANCE, MRPL, CHENNPETRO | Product cracks (esp. diesel) widen in supply shocks | RELIANCE is a conglomerate; telecom/retail dilute it |
| Defence | HAL, BEL, BDL, MAZDOCK, COCHINSHIP | War raises defence spending/order sentiment | Already richly valued; news-driven spikes reverse |
| IT services (via weak INR) | TCS, INFY, HCLTECH, WIPRO | Weaker rupee lifts INR revenue; low oil sensitivity | Depends on US demand, not oil |
| Pharma exporters | SUNPHARMA, DRREDDY, CIPLA | Weak INR helps; defensive | Some solvent/API cost pressure |
| Gold / gold ETFs | GOLDBEES | Safe-haven demand + weak INR | Not a stock, a hedge |
| Coal / alternative energy | COALINDIA, NTPC, TATAPOWER | Substitute fuels; energy security focus | Indirect |
| Shipping (rates spike) | SCI, GESHIP | Tanker freight rates jump when routes are disrupted | Very volatile |

## When crude falls (de-escalation trade)

The table flips: OMCs, aviation, paints, tyres and autos typically rally hardest. Upstream
(ONGC, OIL) and defence give back gains. Write down in advance what de-escalation would look
like (e.g. ceasefire, Hormuz flows back to normal, Brent below a level you pick) so you act
on a plan, not on a headline.

## Quick sensitivity rules of thumb (verify with current estimates)

- Every $10/bbl rise in crude has historically been estimated to widen India's CAD by
  ~0.4–0.5% of GDP and add a few tenths of a percent to CPI inflation, depending on
  pass-through to pump prices. Check current RBI/brokerage estimates; these change.
- Paint and tyre companies have historically held gross margins when crude spikes are short
  and lost 200–400 bps when they persist for 2+ quarters.
